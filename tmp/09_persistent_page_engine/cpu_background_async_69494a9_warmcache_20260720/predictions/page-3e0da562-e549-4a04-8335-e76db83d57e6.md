A point to be noted here is that, in the aforementioned disintegration of  $ \mu_{x,y}^{(\mathrm{ov})} $, one only needs to look at the measures  $ \mu_t $ for  $ t \in \mathrm{Spec}(y) $, and that every such  $ \mu_t $ is supported on  $ \mathrm{Spec}(x) $, where  $ \mathrm{Spec}(x), \mathrm{Spec}(y) \subseteq \mathbb{R} $ are the spectra of the elements  $ x $ and  $ y $, respectively.

The name used for $\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{(ov)}}$ in the present paper is overlap measure of $x$ and $y$. Moreover, if $\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{(ov)}}$ happens to be absolutely continuous with respect to the direct product $\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}$ of the distributions of $x$ and $y$, then we will refer to the Radon-Nikodym derivative of $\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{(ov)}}$ with respect to $\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}$ by calling it the overlap function of $x$ and $y$. We note that $\mu_{x,y}^{\mathrm{(ov)}}$ does not always have to be absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}$ (consider for example $y=x$ where $x$ has absolutely continuous distribution with respect to Lebesgue measure); but this will nevertheless be the case in several cases that are relevant for our calculations.

The motivation for the “overlap” terminology comes from the case when our $\mathcal{A}$ is the algebra of matrices $\mathcal{M}_N(\mathbb{C})$, considered with the normalized trace $\varphi = \frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}$, and when the pair of selfadjoint elements of $\mathcal{A}$ to be considered is thus a pair $(X_N, Y_N)$ of complex Hermitian matrices. What happens here is that upon explicitly writing $X_N$ and $Y_N$ in terms of their eigenvalues and eigenvectors:

 $$ X_{N}=U\mathrm{d i a g}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})U^{*}\mathrm{a n d}Y_{N}=V\mathrm{d i a g}(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N})V^{*}, $$ 

 $$ \mathrm{w i t h~}U=[\vec{u}_{1},\ldots,\vec{u}_{N}]\mathrm{~a n d~}V=[\vec{v}_{1},\ldots,\vec{v}_{N}],\mathrm{~u n i t a r y~m a t r i c e s}, $$ 

one easily finds that

 $$ \mu_{X_{N},Y_{N}}^{(\mathrm{o v})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|\langle\vec{u}_{i},\vec{v}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\delta_{(\lambda_{i},\rho_{j})}. $$ 

Moreover, the above measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the product of distributions (which in this case is just  $ \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{i,j=1}^N \delta_{(\lambda_i, \rho_j)} $, the direct product of the empirical eigenvalue distributions of  $ X_N $ and  $ Y_N $). The Radon-Nikodym derivative at a point  $ (\lambda_i, \rho_j) $ is given by  $ N|\langle \vec{u}_i, \vec{v}_j \rangle|^2 $ – this is precisely the quantity called an overlap in the physics literature.

1.5. Description of results – additive case. We show that the absolute continuity of $\mu_{x,y}^{(\mathrm{ov})}$ with respect to $\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}$ holds when $x=a$ and $y=b+b$ where $a,b$ are freely independent selfadjoint random variables. This result is based on the subordination results of Bianc [12] and regularity properties of free convolutions studied by Belinschi in a series of papers [2, 3, 4, 5]. More precisely we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1. Let $a,b$ be freely independent selfadjoint random variables in a $W^{*}$-probability space $(\mathcal{A},\varphi)$. Let $\mu$ and respectively $\nu$ be the distributions of $a$ and $b$ with respect to $\varphi$, and assume that neither of $\mu,\nu$ is a point mass. Denote by $f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}$ the density of the absolutely continuous part of $\mu\boxplus\nu$ and by $\omega$ the continuation to the real line of the subordination function of $\mu\boxplus\nu$ with respect to $\mu$. Then there exists an open set $U\subset\mathbb{R}$ such that

 $$ \forall s\in\operatorname{S p e c}(a),~\mathrm{o}_{a,a+b}(s,t)=\left\{\begin{matrix}{-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}\operatorname{I m}\left(\frac{1}{\omega(t)-s}\right)\quad}&{{i f~}t\in U,}\\ {\frac{1}{\mu(\{s\})}1_{\omega(t)=s}\quad}&{{i f~t~i s~a n~a t o m~o f~\mu\boxplus\nu,~}}\\ \end{matrix}\right. $$ 

defines $\mu \times \mu \boxplus \nu$-almost everywhere a function $\mathrm{o}_{a,a+b} : \mathrm{Spec}(a) \times \mathrm{Spec}(a+b) \to \mathbb{R}_{+}$. Then $d\mu_{a,a+b}^{(\mathrm{ov})}(s,t) = \mathrm{o}_{a,a+b}(s,t)\mu(ds)\mu \boxplus \nu(dt)$. In particular $\mu_{a,a+b}^{(\mathrm{ov})} \ll \mu \times \mu \boxplus \nu$ and $\mathrm{o}_{a,a+b}$ is the overlap function of $a$ and $a+b$.
