In order to proceed, we need to introduce the idea of divided differences.Definition 4.1 (Divided Differences). The divided differences for a function \( f(x) \) are defined as follows:\[ \begin{array}{cccc} f(x_k) & = & f(x_k), & \\ & f[x_k - 1, x_k] & = & \frac{f[x_k]}{x_k} - \frac{f[x_k - 1]}{x_k - 1}, \\ & f[x_k - 2, x_k - 1, x_k] & = & \frac{f[x_k - 1, x_k]}{x_k} - \frac{f[x_k - 2, x_k - 1]}{x_k - 2}, \\ & f[x_k - 3, x_k - 2, x_k - 1, x_k] & = & \frac{f[x_k - 2, x_k - 1, x_k]}{x_k} - \frac{f[x_k - 3, x_k - 2, x_k - 1]}{x_k - 3}. \\ & & & \end{array} \] The recursive rule for constructing higher-order divided differences is(13) (14) \( f[x_k - j, x_k - j+1, \ldots, x_k] = \frac{f[x_k - j+1, \ldots, x_k]}{x_k - j} - \frac{f[x_k - j, \ldots, x_k - 1]}{x_k - j} \) and is used to construct the divided differences in Table 4.8.The coefficients \( a_k \) of \( P_N(x) \) depend on the values \( f(x_j) \), for \( j = 0, 1, \ldots, k \).
