Proof. Let us first verify (121). For any \(2k + l \leqslant 2m\), \(l \geqslant 1\), we apply (134) with \(K = k + \frac{l}{2}\) and \(L = l + 1\), and using that the neutral term is bounded,

\[
\mathrm{h} _ {q - 2 k - l} ^ {k, l} \lesssim \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} | D _ {x} ^ {k + \frac {l}{2}} f | ^ {2} \left<   v \right> ^ {q - 2 k - l} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime}} \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \sum_ {l ^ {\prime} \leqslant L} | \partial_ {v} ^ {l + 1} f | ^ {2} \left<   v \right> ^ {q - 2 k - l} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime \prime}},
\]

with \(\theta_i' + \theta_i'' < 1\). So, if \(l\) is even, then the \(x\)-integral on the right hand side consists of integer order derivatives and therefore,

\[
\mathrm{h} _ {q - 2 k - l} ^ {k, l} \lesssim \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \mathrm{x} ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime}} \mathrm{d} ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime \prime}}. \tag {123}
\]

If \( l \) is odd, \( l = 2p + 1 \), and hence \( k + p + 1 \leqslant m \), we use interpolation in \( x \) and distribute the weight accordingly,

\[
\begin{array}{l} \int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} | D _ {x} ^ {k + p + \frac {1}{2}} f | ^ {2} \left\langle v \right\rangle^ {q - 2 k - l} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x \leqslant \int_ {\mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \left(\int_ {\Omega} | \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p + 1} f | ^ {2} \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \left(\int_ {\Omega} | \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p} f | ^ {2} \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \mathrm{d} v \\ = \int_ {\mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \left(\int_ {\Omega} \left| \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p + 1} f \right| ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k - 2 p - 2} \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \left(\int_ {\Omega} \left| \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p} f \right| ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k - 2 p} \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \mathrm{d} v \\ \leqslant \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \left| \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p + 1} f \right| ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k - 2 p - 2} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \left| \partial_ {x} ^ {k + p} f \right| ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k - 2 p} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \\ \end{array}
\]

So, if \( k = p = 0 \), then the above is bounded by \( \lesssim \mathrm{x}^{1/2} \), and if \( k + p > 0 \), then by \( \lesssim \mathrm{x} \). In either case we arrive at the same bound (123).

Summing up over \(2k + l \leqslant 2m\), \(l \geqslant 1\), and reindexing the exponents, we obtain

\[
\mathrm{v} \lesssim \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} \mathrm{x} ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime}} \mathrm{d} ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime \prime}},
\]

with \(N\) depending only on the indexes.

As to the mixed term, we have

\[
\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k} \partial_ {x} ^ {\mathbf {k}} f \cdot \partial_ {x} ^ {\mathbf {k} - 1} \partial_ {v} f \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x \leqslant \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} | \partial_ {x} ^ {k} f | ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2} \left(\int_ {\Omega \times \mathbb {R} ^ {n}} | \partial_ {x} ^ {k - 1} \partial_ {v} f | ^ {2} \langle v \rangle^ {q - 2 k} \mathrm{d} v \mathrm{d} x\right) ^ {1 / 2}
\]

using (134) with \(K = k\), \(L = 2k\),

\[
\leqslant \mathrm{h} ^ {1 / 2} \sum_ {i} (\mathrm{h} _ {q - 2 k} ^ {k, 0}) ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime} / 2} (\mathrm{h} _ {q - 2 k} ^ {0, 2 k}) ^ {\theta_ {i} ^ {\prime \prime} / 2} \leqslant \sum_ {i} \mathrm{h} ^ {\theta_ {i}},
\]

where all \(\theta_{i} < 1\). After adding up the terms and relabeling the exponents, we obtain

\[
\mathrm{m} \lesssim \sum_ {i} \mathrm{h} ^ {\theta_ {i}}.
\]
