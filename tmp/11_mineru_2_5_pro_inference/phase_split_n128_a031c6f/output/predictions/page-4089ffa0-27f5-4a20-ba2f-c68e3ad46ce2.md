where \(D^{(n + 1)}(t)\) is the pseudo-derivative of \(D^{(n)}(t)\) and

\[
R _ {1} (t) \ll \frac {1}{(\log t) ^ {C}} \text {and} R _ {n} (t) \ll \frac {1}{t ^ {n - 1} (\log t) ^ {C + 1}} \text {for} n \geq 2.
\]

We get with these results that

\[
\begin{array}{l} \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \pi (u; a, q) \bigg (\frac {n (D ^ {(n)}) ^ {\prime} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 2}} + \frac {t (D ^ {(n + 1)}) ^ {\prime} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 3}} + \frac {D ^ {(n + 1)} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 2}} \bigg) d u \\ = \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \pi (u; a, q) \bigg (\frac {(n + 1) D ^ {(n + 1)} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 2}} + \frac {t D ^ {(n + 2)} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 3}} \bigg) d u \\ + \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \pi (u; a, q) \left(\frac {n R _ {n + 1} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 2}} + \frac {t R _ {n + 2} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 3}}\right) d u. \tag {6.51} \\ \end{array}
\]

The integral with \( D^{(n + 1)} \) and \( D^{(n + 2)} \) is a differentiable function and is the remaining term we need for (6.47). We now show that we can add the integral with \( R_{n + 1} \) and \( R_{n + 2} \) to the error term \( \widetilde{R}_{n + 1}(t) \). Using that \( \frac{1}{2}\log t\leq \log (t / u)\leq \log t \) for \( 1\leq u\leq \sqrt{t} \), we get for \( n\geq 1 \)

\[
\begin{array}{l} \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \pi (u; a, q) \left(\frac {n R _ {n + 1} (t / u)}{u ^ {n + 2}}\right) d u \ll \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \frac {u}{\log u} \left(\frac {n}{u ^ {n + 2} \left(\frac {t}{u}\right) ^ {n} (\log (t / u)) ^ {C + 1}}\right) d u \\ \ll \frac {n}{t ^ {n} (\log t) ^ {C + 1}} \int_ {2} ^ {\sqrt {t}} \frac {d u}{u \log u} \ll \frac {\log \log t}{t ^ {n} (\log t) ^ {C + 1}} \ll \frac {1}{t ^ {n} (\log t) ^ {C}}. \\ \end{array}
\]

Similarly for the integral with \( R_{n+2} \) and also for \( n = 0 \). This completes the proof of (6.47) and that \( g(t) \) is a strange function.
