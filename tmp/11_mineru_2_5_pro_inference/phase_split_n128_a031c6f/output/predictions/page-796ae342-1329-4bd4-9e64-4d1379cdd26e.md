The first and the third named authors have proved in [HW25] that, under mild assumptions on the location of poles, the  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)} \)  satisfy linear and quadratic loop equations and obey blobbed topological recursion [BS17]. Loop equations in the quartic analogue of the Kontsevich model lead so such  \( \omega_{n}^{(0)} \)  for the special involution  \( \iota z = -z \) . See [HW25, BHW22].

Although surprising at first sight, \(\omega_2^{(0)}\) is symmetric: one has \(B(w,\iota z) = B(\iota w,z)\) and \(B(w,z) = B(\iota w,\iota z)\). This paper addresses the important question whether all \(\omega_n^{(0)}(z_1,\dots,z_n)\) defined by (1) and (2) are symmetric in their arguments \(z_i\). This symmetry is by no means obvious; it has not been proved so far. We will show that symmetry is reduced to the following purely combinatorial statement about integer partitions into a given number of parts:

Theorem 1. Let  \( \mathsf{P}_{k}(n) \)  be the set of partitions of an integer  \( n \geq 1 \)  into  \( 1 \leq k \leq n \)  parts. For any given integers  \( (s, k, l) \)  with  \( k, l \geq 0 \)  and  \( s \geq \max(k + l, 1) \)  and any given partition  \( \nu \in \mathsf{P}_{s}(2s - k - l) \)  one has

\[
\begin{array}{l} s! = \sum_{\substack{(r - l) + p_{1} + \ldots +p_{l} = s\\ r - l\geq k, p_{1},\ldots ,p_{l}\geq 1}}\sum_{\mu \in \mathsf{P}_{r - l}(2r - k - 2l)}\sum_{\rho_{1}\in \mathsf{P}_{p_{1}}(2p_{1} - 1)}\dots \sum_{\rho_{l}\in \mathsf{P}_{p_{l}}(2p_{l} - 1)} \\ \times \binom {\nu} {\mu , \rho_ {1}, \dots , \rho_ {l}} (r - l)! (p _ {1} - 1)! \dots (p _ {l} - 1)!. \tag {3} \\ \end{array}
\]

\(By\left(\nu_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}}\right)\) we denote the multinomial coefficient of partitions,

\[
\begin{array}{l} \binom{\nu}{\mu_ {1}, \dots , \mu_ {l}} = \lim _ {N \to \infty} \prod_ {k = 1} ^ {N} \binom{h _ {k}}{g _ {k, 1}, \dots , g _ {k, l}} \\ i f \quad \nu = (\dots , \underbrace {2 , \dots , 2} _ {h _ {2}}, \underbrace {1 , \dots , 1} _ {h _ {1}}), \quad \mu_ {i} = (\dots , \underbrace {2 , \dots , 2} _ {g _ {2, i}}, \underbrace {1 , \dots , 1} _ {g _ {1, i}}), \\ \end{array}
\]

where \(\binom{h_k}{g_{k,1},\dots,g_{k,l}}\) are the usual multinomial coefficients which vanish unless \(h_k = g_{k,1} + \dots +g_{k,l}\).

We give two examples at the end of sec. 5 and a proof of this theorem in sec. 6. This theorem is a main ingredient of the proof of the symmetry:

Theorem 2. All differentials \(\omega_{n}^{(0)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\) defined by (1) and (2) are symmetric in their arguments \(z_{i}\).

Theorem 2 is proved by reduction to Theorem 1 in sec. 5.
