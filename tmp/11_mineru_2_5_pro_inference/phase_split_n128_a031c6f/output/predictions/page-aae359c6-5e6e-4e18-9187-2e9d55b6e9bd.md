2.9. Fully faithful centralizing functors. In order to use the technology developed above, we must have some way of constructing objects of \(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}^b (\mathcal{M}))\). Many examples (see §3) can be provided with the following relatively elementary result.

Theorem 2.21. Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a \(\mathbb{k}\)-linear monoidal category and \(\mathcal{M}\) an \((\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})\) \(\star\)-bimodule category, and let \(Z\in\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M})\) be given. Assume that \(L_{Z},R_{Z}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M})\) are fully faithful and have the same essential image. Then there is a monoidal autoequivalence \(\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\) such that \(Z\star X\simeq\Phi(X)\star Z\), naturally in \(X\in\mathcal{A}\). In other words, \(Z\) has the structure of an object in the Drinfeld centralizer \(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\Phi\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M}))\), where \(\Phi\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M})\) denotes \(\mathcal{K}^{b}(\mathcal{M})\) with left action twisted by \(\Phi\).

Proof. Without loss of generality, let us assume that \(\mathcal{A}\) is strict monoidal. Let \(\mathcal{N} \subset \mathcal{K}^b(\mathcal{M})\) be the essential image of \(L_Z\) and \(R_Z\). Then \(L_Z, R_Z\) restrict to equivalences of categories \(\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{N}\), and we may choose an inverse functor \(R_Z^{-1}\) and define \(\Phi := R_Z^{-1} \circ L_Z\). The fact that \(Z \star X \simeq \Phi(X) \star Z\), naturally in \(X\) is true by construction. There is an isomorphism \(\psi_{X_1, X_2} \colon \Phi(X_1 \star X_2) \xrightarrow{\cong} \Phi(X_1) \star \Phi(X_2)\), defined by commutativity of the diagram

\[
\begin{array}{c} \Phi (X _ {1}) \star \Phi (X _ {2}) \star Z \xleftarrow {\mathrm{id} _ {\Phi (X _ {1})} \star \tau_ {X _ {2}}} \Phi (X _ {1}) \star Z \star X _ {2} \\ \psi_ {X _ {1}, X _ {2}} \star \mathrm{id} _ {Z} \Bigg \uparrow \\ \Phi (X _ {1} \star X _ {2}) \star Z \xrightarrow {(\tau_ {X _ {1} \star X _ {2}}) ^ {- 1}} Z \star X _ {1} \star X _ {2} \end{array} \Bigg \uparrow \tau_ {X _ {1}} \star \mathrm{id} _ {X _ {2}} \tag {2.46}
\]

Where the solid arrows alternate between homotopy equivalences of the form \( R_Z(\Phi(X)) \simeq L_Z(X) \), and associator isomorphisms. Observe that, a priori the above diagram defines a homotopy equivalence \( \Phi(X_1 \star X_2) \simeq \Phi(X_1) \star \Phi(X_2) \). But since \( X_i \) here are objects of \( \mathcal{A} \), all homotopies are zero for degree reasons. Moreover, naturality of the isomorphism \( R_Z \circ \Phi \simeq L_Z \) and of the associator implies that the isomorphism \( \Phi(X_1 \star X_2) \cong \Phi(X_1) \star \Phi(X_2) \) is natural in \( X_1, X_2 \in \mathcal{A} \).

To complete the proof that \(\Phi\) is a monoidal functor, one would need to prove commutativity of the diagram

\[
\begin{array}{c} \Phi (X _ {1} \star X _ {2} \star X _ {3}) \xrightarrow {\Phi (X _ {1}) \star \Phi (X _ {2} \star X _ {3})} \Phi (X _ {1}) \star \Phi (X _ {2}) \star \Phi (X _ {3}) \\ \xrightarrow {\Phi (X _ {1} \star X _ {2}) \star \Phi (X _ {3})} \end{array} \tag {2.47}
\]
