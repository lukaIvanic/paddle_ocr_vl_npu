5.1. Expansion in the q-aspect. Since  \( \pi_{1} \)  and  \( \pi_{2} \)  are cuspidal automorphic representations, We note that  \( \Phi_{1} = \varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}^{q} \)  is a rapid-decay function which is invariant under the congruence group  \( \mathbf{K}_{0}(\mathfrak{uvq}) \) , we apply Plancherel formula (See [Zac20, Theorem 2.8]) to the well-defined inner product (5.2) in the space of forms of level mnq. We have the following decomposition of the considered period

\[
\mathcal {P} _ {\mathfrak {q}} (\mathfrak {l}, \Phi , \Phi) = \mathcal {G} _ {\mathfrak {q}} (\mathfrak {l}, \Phi , \Phi) + \mathcal {C} _ {1}, \tag {5.3}
\]

where the generic part is given by

\[
\begin{array}{l} \mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{l},\Phi ,\Phi) = \sum_{\substack{\pi \text{cuspidal}\\ c(\pi)|\mathfrak{uvq}}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{l})\sum_{\psi \in \mathcal{B}(\pi ,\mathfrak{uvq})}\left|\left\langle \varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}^{\mathfrak{q}},\psi \right\rangle \right|^{2} \\ + \sum_{\substack{\chi \in \widehat{F^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}}\\ \mathrm{c}(\chi)^{2}|\mathfrak{w}\mathfrak{q}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda_{\chi ,it}(\mathfrak{l})\sum_{\psi_{it}\in \mathcal{B}(\chi ,\chi^{-1},it,\mathfrak{w}\mathfrak{q})}\left|\langle \varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}^{\mathfrak{q}},\mathrm{E}(\psi_{it})\rangle \right|^{2}\frac{dt}{4\pi}. \tag{5.4} \\ \end{array}
\]

Moreover, the constant term  \( C_{1} \)  is the one-dimensional contribution (constant term) which appears only if both  \( \pi_{1} \)  and  \( \pi_{2} \)  are cuspidal and there exists finite many quadratic characters  \( \chi \)  (depending on the number field F) of  \( F^{\times}\backslash A_{F}^{1} \)  such that  \( \pi_{1}\simeq\pi_{2}\otimes\chi \)  ( \( \chi=1 \)  if  \( \pi_{1}=\pi_{2} \)  for example). The cardinality of such quadratic character  \( \chi \)  is  \( O_{\varepsilon,F}((uvq)^{\varepsilon})=O_{\varepsilon,F}(q^{\varepsilon}) \) . For such a quadratic character  \( \chi \) , by applying the Hecke relation (3.5), we have the following:

\[
\begin{array}{l} \mathcal {C} _ {1} = V _ {F} ^ {- 1} \cdot \langle \mathrm{T} _ {\mathrm{l}} (\Phi), \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle \langle \varphi_ {\chi}, \Phi \rangle = \frac {\chi (\mathrm{l}) \deg (\mathrm{T} _ {\mathrm{l}})}{V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \left\langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2} ^ {\mathrm{q}}, \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac {\zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathrm{l}) \deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathrm{l}}\right)}{\zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \frac {1}{q ^ {1 / 2}} \left(\left\langle \varphi_ {1} \cdot \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak {q}} \varphi_ {2}\right), \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle - \frac {1}{q _ {1}} \cdot \left\langle \varphi_ {1} \cdot \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak {q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}} \varphi_ {2}\right), \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle\right) \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac {\zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathfrak {l}) \deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak {l}}\right)}{\zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \frac {1}{q ^ {1 / 2}} \left(\lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} (\mathfrak {q}) - \frac {1}{q _ {1}} \lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} \left(\mathfrak {q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}\right)\right) \cdot \langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2}, \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac {\zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathfrak {l}) \deg (\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak {l}})}{q \zeta_ {\mathfrak {q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \left(\lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} (\mathfrak {q}) - \frac {1}{q _ {1}} \lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} \left(\mathfrak {q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}\right)\right) \right| ^ {2} \cdot | \langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2}, \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle | ^ {2}, \\ \end{array}
\]

(5.5)

where  \( T_{q} \)  is the Hecke operator and  \( \zeta_{\mathfrak{q}}(s):=\prod_{v|\mathfrak{q}}\zeta_{F_{v}}(s) \)  is the partial Dedekind zeta function. If the integral ideal q is squarefree, i.e. m=1, we define  \( T_{q_{1}^{m-2}}:=0 \)  and  \( \lambda_{\pi_{2}}\left(\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{m-2}\right):=0 \) . Hence,  \( T_{q_{1}^{m-2}}\varphi_{2}=0 \) . Moreover, the degree of the Hecke operator  \( T_{l} \)  is defined by

\[
\deg (\mathrm{T} _ {\mathrm{l}}) := \frac {1}{\ell^ {1 / 2}} \sum_ {0 \leqslant k \leqslant \frac {n}{2}} \gamma_ {n - 2 k} = \ell^ {1 / 2} \frac {\zeta_ {F _ {\mathrm{p}}} (1)}{\zeta_ {F _ {\mathrm{p}}} (n + 1)} \leqslant \ell^ {1 / 2} \zeta_ {F _ {\mathrm{p}}} (1). \tag {5.6}
\]

Since \(\pi_2\) is \(\theta_2\)-tempered at all the finite places, we have \(|\lambda_{\pi_2}(\mathfrak{q})| \leqslant (m + 1) \cdot q^{\theta_2}\). Applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\[
\mathcal {C} _ {1} \ll_ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} (\ell q) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac {\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {1 - 2 \theta_ {2}}} \ll_ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} (\ell q) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac {\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {2 5 / 3 2}}
\]

since \(0 \leqslant \theta_{2} \leqslant \frac{7}{64}\). Hence we can conclude

\[
\mathcal {P} _ {\mathfrak {q}} (\mathfrak {l}, \Phi , \Phi) = \mathcal {G} _ {\mathfrak {q}} (\mathfrak {l}, \Phi , \Phi) + O _ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} \left((\ell q) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac {\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {1 - 2 \theta_ {2}}}\right), \tag {5.7}
\]

where we recall that \(\Phi\) is defined as (5.1).
