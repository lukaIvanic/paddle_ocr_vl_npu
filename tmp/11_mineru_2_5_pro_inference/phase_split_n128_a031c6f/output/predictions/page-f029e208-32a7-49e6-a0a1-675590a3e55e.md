DEFINITION 2.8.  \( \tilde{h} \)  in Theorem 2.7 is called the Koszul-Vinbergization of a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure h on  \( (A,\phi_{0}) \) .

Now we define a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold as a symmetric analogue of a Jacobi manifold.

DEFINITION 2.9. A Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on an affine manifold  \( (M,\nabla) \)  is a pair  \( (h,E) \)  in  \( \Gamma(S^{2}TM)\times\mathfrak{X}(M) \)  satisfying,

for any \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) in \(\Omega^1(M)\),

(i) \((\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\alpha}h)(\beta ,\gamma) - \langle \alpha ,E\rangle h(\beta ,\gamma) = (\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}h)(\alpha ,\gamma) - \langle \beta ,E\rangle h(\alpha ,\gamma),\)

(ii) \((\nabla_{E}h)(\beta ,\gamma) - \langle \gamma ,\nabla_{h^{\sharp}\beta}E\rangle +\langle \beta ,E\rangle \langle \gamma ,E\rangle = 0,\)

(iii) \(\nabla_{E}E = 0\).

\((M,\nabla ,h,E)\) is called a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg manifold.

Let \((M,\nabla)\) be an affine manifold. As previously stated in Example 1.8, \((TM\oplus\mathbb{R},\bar{\nabla},\mathrm{pr}_{1})\) is a left-symmetric algebroid over \(M\). Furthermore, because of Example 1.13 and Proposition 2.2, \(((TM\oplus\mathbb{R},\bar{\nabla},\mathrm{pr}_{1}),(0,1))\) is a Jacobi-left-symmetric algebroid over \(M\).

PROPOSITION 2.10. Let  \( (M,\nabla) \)  be an affine manifold. Identify  \( (h,E) \)  in  \( \Gamma(S^{2}TM)\times\mathfrak{X}(M) \)  with H in  \( \Gamma(S^{2}(TM\oplus\mathbb{R})) \)  by  \( H=h+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\otimes E+E\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \) . Under this identification, the followings are equivalent;

(1) \((h,E)\) is a Jacobi-Koszul-Vinberg structure on \((M,\nabla)\)

(2) \(\llbracket H, H \rrbracket_{TM \oplus \mathbb{R}}^{(0,1)} = 0\).
